The problem

When the corridor changes, decisions get harder.

Weather disruption on the east–west freight corridor can affect shipments, stock and delivery plans. Businesses need to understand what is known, which information is current and where the gaps are. Corridor Lifeline starts by bringing those signals together so people can review the situation with clearer context.

  • Scattered information. Weather warnings and rail announcements arrive through separate sources.
  • Changing conditions. A useful update needs its source and timestamp, not just a status label.
  • Human decisions. Freight teams still need to review official information and decide what to do next.

The direction

Start with visibility.
Build from evidence.

Only first-stage monitoring and draft summaries are implemented today. Later capabilities remain proposed.

Detection and context · Prototype

Bring configured weather and verified rail information into an explainable view, keeping data quality visible.

Review the information · Prototype

Prepare a plain-language email draft with reasons, source timestamps and visible gaps for human review.

Subscriber alerts · Proposed

Develop delivery of reviewed updates after validating source coverage, permissions and delivery behaviour.

Paperwork support · Proposed

Explore draft emergency documentation based on verified requirements, with human review before submission.

Validate before expanding

Advance-warning performance still needs verified historical testing. No lead time or closure-prediction accuracy has been demonstrated.
