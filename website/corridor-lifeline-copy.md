## Problem

Eyebrow: THE PROBLEM

Headline: When the corridor changes, decisions get harder.

Body: Weather disruption on the east–west freight corridor can affect shipments, stock and delivery plans. Businesses need to understand what is known, which information is current and where the gaps are. Corridor Lifeline starts by bringing those signals together so people can review the situation with clearer context.

Three supporting points:

- **Scattered information.** Weather warnings and rail announcements arrive through separate sources.
- **Changing conditions.** A useful update needs its source and timestamp, not just a status label.
- **Human decisions.** Freight teams still need to review official information and decide what to do next.

## Product

Eyebrow: CORRIDOR LIFELINE

Headline: Understand the signals. See the gaps.

Intro: The current local Python prototype has three parts:

- **Gather the inputs.** Read configured BOM weather observations and warning feeds alongside manually verified rail-status information.
- **Explain the score.** Apply visible rules and show the reasons behind each result, with source timestamps and missing or stale inputs.
- **Prepare a summary.** Generate plain-language email drafts for a person to review.

Visible qualification: The score is an illustrative rule score, not a probability or a prediction of closure. The prototype does not send alerts, submit permits or provide complete corridor coverage. It does not generate an automatic all-clear when information is missing.

## Journey / plan

Eyebrow: THE DIRECTION

Headline: Start with visibility. Build from evidence.

Intro: The Corridor Lifeline plan connects three jobs. Only the first-stage monitoring and draft summaries are implemented today; later capabilities remain proposed.

1. **Detection and context — prototype stage.** Bring configured weather and verified rail information into an explainable view, keeping data quality visible.
2. **Subscriber alerts — proposed.** Develop a dependable way to deliver reviewed updates, after validating source coverage, permissions and delivery behaviour.
3. **Paperwork support — proposed.** Explore draft emergency documentation based on verified requirements, with human review before any submission.

Closing line: Advance-warning performance still needs to be tested against verified historical data. No lead time or closure-prediction accuracy has been demonstrated.
