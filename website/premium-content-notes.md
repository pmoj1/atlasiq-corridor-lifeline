# Premium Corridor Lifeline narrative

Product advisory for the lead · 13 October 2026

Audience assumption: freight operators and businesses whose shipments depend on the east–west corridor. Their first decision is whether this idea addresses their needs and merits a conversation. Lead with the mission, then the proposed workflow, then concise evidence of build stage.

## Suggested page sequence and copy

**Hero**

Badge: Corridor Lifeline · In development

Headline: A lifeline for the freight that keeps WA moving.

Body: AtlasIQ is developing Corridor Lifeline to bring changing weather, corridor conditions and the next steps into clearer focus — so freight businesses can plan their response with better information.

Actions: Explore the plan / Register interest

**Stakes / mission**

Headline: Every shipment carries a promise.

Body: A delivery expected. A shelf to restock. A business depending on what arrives next. When conditions change on the east–west corridor, the impact reaches far beyond the track. Our mission is to give the people moving freight a clearer picture of what is happening, and a better starting point for deciding what comes next.

**Three-part vision**

Section label: The Corridor Lifeline plan

Headline: From signal to response.

Intro: One focused direction: connect detection, alerts and emergency paperwork support in a workflow built around human decisions.

- **01 / Detect.** Bring weather observations, warnings and verified rail updates into an understandable picture of changing conditions.
- **02 / Alert — planned.** Deliver relevant updates to subscribed businesses, so teams can review the situation and consider their next move.
- **03 / Prepare — planned.** Help draft emergency paperwork using verified requirements, ready for a person to check before submission.

**Principle / interlude**

Headline: Clarity when conditions change.

Body: A useful update shows what it knows, where it came from and how recently it was checked. Missing information stays visible. The decision stays with you.

**Focused build status**

Headline: The first foundation is working.

Body: The current local prototype combines configured weather inputs with manually verified rail status, applies transparent scoring rules and prepares plain-language email drafts. The next work is to validate broader coverage and historical performance, then develop subscriber delivery and paperwork support.

Small visible note: Early prototype. Scores explain configured signals; closure prediction and advance-warning performance have not been demonstrated. Drafts are not sent automatically.

If a demonstration is pictured: label it “Illustrative concept”. If historical demos are mentioned: “Offline scenarios use illustrative weather inputs; they demonstrate software behaviour, not predictive accuracy.”

**Founder**

Label: Solo founder

Headline: One founder. One focused mission.

Name: Jay-deane Huni

Body: I’m building AtlasIQ around Corridor Lifeline: a practical starting point for understanding disruption on the east–west freight corridor. The aim is to build evidence, listen to the people who depend on it and develop the next capabilities with care.

**Closing invitation**

Headline: Help shape the next chapter.

Body: If your business depends on the corridor, we’d like to understand the information you need when conditions change. Register interest in future updates, or start a conversation.

CTA: Register interest

Interest-page note: Corridor Lifeline is in development. Expressing interest does not subscribe you to operational alerts or guarantee pilot access.

Keep the existing email-based interest flow honestly described: “Open an email to register interest.” Opening an email draft must not display “You're on the list”.

## Editorial and visual acceptance

- Entire narrative stays within Corridor Lifeline; remove legacy customs, order-entry and multi-founder positioning.
- Sole founder is Jay-deane Huni, confirmed in the plan. No invented biography, qualifications or partnerships.
- Detection → alerts → emergency paperwork is easy to understand before the build-status section. Planned badges remain next to unimplemented capability cards.
- No promised lead time, prediction accuracy, automatic permit submission, deployed dashboard, commercial feed access, customer logos or performance statistics.
- Do not publish the strategy PDF's historical incident numbers, competitor exclusivity or “would have caught” claims as facts.
- Photos convey freight, distance and weather. Do not identify supplied international stock as WA, the Nullarbor or real closure footage. Use accurate neutral alt text and any required stock credits.
- Product/score illustrations show a concept label and must not appear to report live conditions. Do not pair “live” status with a decorative timestamp or healthy-source indicators.
- Keep technical implementation detail out of the hero and product story. One compact build-status section provides the necessary distinction without repeating disclaimers throughout.
- Existing verified contact details and visual brand remain the lead's integration inputs; no external changes or new collection services are authorised by this note.
